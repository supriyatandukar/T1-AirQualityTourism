# Nepal T1: Climate Change & Air Quality in the Himalayas
 
## Project Summary
Nepal's dry winter months bring both its worst air quality and its busiest tourist season.
This project integrates air quality, climate, and tourism data to show how seasonal
pollution spikes in the Kathmandu Valley and beyond directly overlap with - and threaten -
Nepal's peak tourism period.
 
**Target audience:** Department of Environment, Nepal Tourism Board
 
## Key Finding
Monthly PM2.5 averages in dry-season months (Nov–Jan) reach 65–94 µg/m³ - 4 to 6 times
the WHO guideline (15 µg/m³) - while monsoon months (Jul–Aug) average 15-17 µg/m³.
This pollution spike overlaps almost exactly with Nepal's peak tourist arrival months
(Oct–Dec), posing a risk to the country's "clean Himalaya" tourism image.
